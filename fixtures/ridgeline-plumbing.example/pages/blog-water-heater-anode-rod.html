How to tell if your water heater’s anode rod needs replacing

Inside most tank water heaters is a metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. It is called the anode rod, and when it is used up the tank itself starts to rust from the inside.

To check it, turn off power or gas and the cold supply, drain a few gallons and loosen the hex head on top of the tank. A rod that is pitted, thin or coated in calcium is due for replacement. A rod that still looks like a solid bar has plenty of life left.

Replacing a rod is a modest job compared with replacing a tank. If your heater is past its middle years and the rod is gone, an inspection will tell you whether to renew the rod or plan for a new heater.
